## Where This CV Is Genuinely Ambiguous

Six of my eleven years are SOC and detection work, two of my five certifications are GIAC
analyst certifications, and a large part of my Skills section is Splunk, Python and detection
content. Read on its own, this is a detection engineer's CV and it would sit comfortably in a
cybersecurity team rather than a network team.

The reason I still call myself a network security engineer is that the controls and the sensors
are mine to build and operate, and the detections I write are network detections that depend on
understanding routing, TLS and DNS at a level a general SOC analyst usually does not have. But
this is a genuine borderline rather than a defensive claim, and I would not argue with an employer
who filed me under detection engineering instead.

What I am clearly not: an endpoint or identity specialist, a malware reverse engineer, or a
pentester. I have never written an exploit and my EDR knowledge is that of a consumer of its
alerts.
