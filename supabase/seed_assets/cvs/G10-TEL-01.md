## Profile

Thirteen years in mobile networks, mostly on the packet core and the transport underneath it.
This is a corner of networking with its own vocabulary and its own failure modes: a misconfigured
APN does not drop a link, it silently denies data service to a subscriber segment, and you find
out from a customer care queue rather than from a monitoring alert. I have run 3G, 4G and now 5G
core, and I have been through two vendor swaps, which is the experience I would say has taught me
the most.

## Two Failures Worth Recording

The 2019 signalling storm was partly mine. We had modelled reattach behaviour for a handset
population and applied the same assumptions to the M2M APN, where a hundred thousand meters power
on within the same thirty seconds. The core survived; the customer experience for that region did
not, for about forty minutes. The backoff work described above came out of it, and the lesson I
took is that "the same technology" and "the same traffic profile" are unrelated statements.

Second: during the 2022 HSS migration I signed off a batch without checking that the roaming
profile fields had mapped correctly, because they were not in the primary test set. About 8,000
subscribers lost outbound roaming for two days. It was recoverable, it was embarrassing, and it
was a failure of my own test design rather than of the vendor's tooling.
