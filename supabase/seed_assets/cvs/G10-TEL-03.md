## Experience

### Senior Telecom Engineer, Private Networks | Bosch Vietnam (manufacturing IT), Binh Duong | May 2021 - Present

- Own the private 5G network at a manufacturing site - a standalone core on-premises, 14 indoor
  small cells, and roughly 900 connected devices split between AGVs, handheld terminals, machine
  vision cameras and sensor gateways.
- Deployed the network from a greenfield state over about fourteen months: spectrum arrangement,
  radio planning across a 40,000 square metre plant with a lot of metal in it, core deployment on
  an on-premises Kubernetes platform, and integration into the factory IT network.
- Replaced the Wi-Fi network the AGVs had been running on. The problem was not throughput; it was
  handover - the AGVs lost association crossing between access points at aisle intersections,
  about eleven stoppages a week. Post-migration that is under one a month, and that number is the
  entire business case.
- Designed the latency budget end to end for the motion control use case: 12 ms target from
  sensor to controller, split across radio, transport, core user plane and the application. Meeting
  it required moving the UPF to the plant edge and arguing an application team out of a polling
  design.
- Own the device side more than a carrier engineer would: 5G modules (Quectel and Telit) on
  customer hardware, AT command provisioning, SIM and eSIM lifecycle for 900 devices, firmware
  update strategy for the modules, and the antenna placement work, which involves a ladder.
- Built the telemetry and analytics for network quality - per-device RSRP, SINR and throughput
  into a time series store, with a dashboard the production team reads. Roughly 40 million
  measurements a day. This is where the data-engineering half of my skills comes from.
- Work with the OT side constantly: Profinet and EtherCAT segments behind gateways, and the
  question of what may cross between OT and IT, which is a security conversation before it is a
  network one.
