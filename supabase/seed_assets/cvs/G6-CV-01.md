## Experience

### Senior Computer Vision Engineer | VinFast, Hai Phong and Binh Duong | Oct 2022 - Present
- Visual inspection on the battery-pack assembly line: weld-quality classification, connector
  seating, and label verification at six stations.
- Weld inspection model runs at 45ms per frame on a Jetson AGX Orin, 12 stations sharing three
  devices. False-negative rate 0.4% at a 3.1% false-positive rate, tuned deliberately in that
  direction because a missed bad weld is a recall and a false alarm is 20 seconds of an
  operator's time.
- Cut the false-positive rate from 9% to 3.1% over four months. Almost all of it was lighting: we
  replaced ring lights with a dome diffuser at two stations and retrained. The model architecture
  never changed.
- Built the data-collection rig and the labelling workflow. 340,000 labelled images to date,
  reviewed by two quality inspectors on rotation. Defect classes are agreed with the process
  engineers, not invented by me.
- Handle drift: the model degrades when the supplier of a component changes and the surface
  finish shifts. Monthly comparison of embedding distributions against a reference set, with a
  documented retrain trigger. Fired four times, three of them supplier changes.
- Wrote the deployment stack the line uses - Triton on the edge devices, TensorRT INT8 engines,
  a watchdog that fails the station open rather than closed, and a local ring buffer so that when
  the network drops we do not lose the evidence images.
- Trained two maintenance technicians to do first-line diagnosis. Callouts to me dropped by
  roughly two thirds.

### Computer Vision Engineer | Samsung Electronics Vietnam (SEVT), Thai Nguyen | Feb 2019 - Sep 2022
- AOI for smartphone module assembly. Surface defect detection on camera modules and display
  glass.
- Anomaly detection with a patch-based feature-matching approach rather than supervised
  classification, because defect classes kept appearing that nobody had seen before. Recall on
  the held-out defect set 96%, and importantly it flagged two novel defect types in the first
  quarter that a supervised model would have passed.
- Worked to a 22ms cycle-time budget. Half my engineering time in that role was optimisation:
  TensorRT, batching, cutting a redundant colour conversion, and eventually moving the
  preprocessing onto the frame grabber.
- Standardised the image acquisition specification across four lines - camera model, lens,
  lighting geometry, exposure. Before that every line had different hardware and no model
  transferred.
- Documentation and change control to Samsung's process standards. Slow, and it taught me habits
  I have kept.
