## Profile

Performance test engineer, eight years, mostly digital banking and payments. The job is three
things people tend to conflate: building a workload model that resembles real traffic, running
it against an environment whose relationship to production is documented rather than assumed,
and then finding out where the time actually goes once the number comes back. Currently the
senior performance engineer for a bank's digital channels, reporting into engineering rather
than into a separate QA function.

## Work Experience

### Senior Performance Test Engineer | Sacombank, Ho Chi Minh City | Apr 2022 - Present
- Performance testing for internet banking, mobile banking, and the payment gateway in front of
  card, QR, and interbank transfer traffic.
- Rebuilt the workload model from six months of production transaction logs instead of a
  business estimate - eight transaction types weighted by observed volume, with a separate
  profile for the salary-day peak and another for Tet. The previous flat model understated load
  on the QR payment path by roughly 3x.
- The 2023 payday readiness test is the piece of work I would point at: target 2,400 TPS on the
  transfer and QR payment paths, sustained for three hours. First run degraded past 1,400 TPS
  on database connection exhaustion in a service nobody had load-tested before. A pooling fix
  plus an index change got the system to 2,700 TPS at p95 380ms, and the real payday peak
  landed around 2,100 TPS with no incident.
- Run the soak tests, which is where the memory leaks live. Two found in two years: a leaking
  session cache in the OTP service that took 19 hours to become visible, and an unbounded retry
  queue that only appeared under a partial dependency failure.
- Own the performance NFRs, written with the product owner before development as a percentile
  target for a named transaction at a named load - "fast enough" is not testable and stopped
  being an acceptable requirement two years ago.
- Set up continuous performance smoke tests in k6 in the CI pipeline: 10 critical endpoints,
  four minutes, p95 compared against the previous release with a 15% regression threshold. It
  has caught five regressions before they reached the release branch, including an N+1 query
  that would have been invisible at test volume.
- Mentor two performance testers and train functional testers to run and read a basic load
  test, so that not every routine check has to come through me.
