## Profile

Sixteen years, the last seven in enterprise architecture and the last three heading the function
at a commercial bank. Enterprise architecture has a reputation for producing artefacts nobody
uses, and it is largely deserved: the discipline fails when it becomes a review gate rather than
a decision-support function. I run it the other way round - my team is measured on whether
project teams consult us before a decision, not on whether we caught them afterwards. We are
consulted on about 70 per cent of significant initiatives, which was 25 per cent when I arrived.

## Where This Function Has Failed Under Me

For the first eighteen months at SeABank my team produced a target architecture that project
teams routed around. The documents were good. The problem was that we were engaged at gate review,
by which point a team had already committed to a design and our input could only be an obstacle.
Moving two architects to sit inside delivery teams - losing central control, which I resisted -
was what changed the consultation rate. I was wrong about that for a year and a half.

I also over-invested in the application portfolio's completeness in 2022, chasing the last 15 per
cent of a 214-application inventory when the top 40 applications carried nearly all the decisions.
Roughly four months of an architect's time bought very little.
